Storage Lifecycle Policies: Archive Before You Pay

A schema-level object that moves old rows to cheap storage or deletes them, on a daily schedule, without a warehouse. The savings are real; two of the choices are irreversible.

How It Works

Two objects and one daily job. No warehouse, no task, no orchestration.

Shared Compute

Policies execute on Snowflake-managed shared compute, not your warehouse. There is nothing to size and nothing to suspend.

First Run Is Fast

Attaching a policy triggers a run within a few minutes. After that it is once daily.

Large Jobs Chunk

Big operations process incrementally and may span several daily runs. A policy that has not finished is normal, not stuck.

Expire or Archive

Expiration deletes permanently. Archival moves rows to a cheaper tier for a set period, then expires them.

Applies to standard tables, dynamic tables, and interactive tables that do not auto-refresh. To evaluate your expression, Snowflake temporarily bypasses governance policies on the table — worth knowing if you are reasoning about masking or row access in an audit. Dynamic tables Interactive tables

COOL vs COLD

The tier decision is about retrieval time and cloud availability, not just price.

Retrieval costHigherLower
One-time archiving costSameSame
Cloud availabilityAWS, Azure, GCPAWS and GCP only
Max files per restore—1 million

Choose COOL When

Anyone might need the data back on short notice, or the account is on Azure, where COLD is not available. Instant retrieval is the whole value.

Choose COLD When

The data is retained for compliance and realistically will never be read. A 48-hour retrieval window has to be acceptable to whoever might ask for it — confirm that with them, not for them.

Check the cloud first. Expiration policies work on AWS, Azure, and GCP. COOL works on all three. COLD is AWS and GCP only. Designing a COLD tier for an Azure account is a dead end, and the feature is unavailable in the People’s Republic of China entirely. Cloud provider support

Creating a Policy

The policy is a boolean expression over a column. Rows that return true are acted on.

-- Expiration only: delete rows older than 7 years.
CREATE STORAGE LIFECYCLE POLICY expire_old_events
  AS (event_ts TIMESTAMP) RETURNS BOOLEAN ->
    event_ts < DATEADD(day, -2555, CURRENT_TIMESTAMP());

-- Archive then expire: COLD for 6 years, then gone.
-- COLD requires ARCHIVE_FOR_DAYS >= 180.
CREATE STORAGE LIFECYCLE POLICY archive_then_expire_finance
  AS (ts TIMESTAMP) RETURNS BOOLEAN ->
    ts < DATEADD(day, -365, CURRENT_TIMESTAMP())
  ARCHIVE_FOR_DAYS = 2190
  ARCHIVE_TIER = COLD;

-- Attach it. One policy can serve many tables.
ALTER TABLE finance.ledger.transactions
  ADD STORAGE LIFECYCLE POLICY archive_then_expire_finance ON (TXN_TS);

Reusable

A policy is a schema-level object. Attach the same one to many tables and change the rule in one place.

Minimums Are Enforced

COOL needs ARCHIVE_FOR_DAYS >= 90, COLD needs >= 180. Omit ARCHIVE_FOR_DAYS and you have an expiration-only policy.

Any Expression

Age is the common case, but the expression is arbitrary boolean logic over the column you bind.

Inspect It

SHOW STORAGE LIFECYCLE POLICIES and DESCRIBE report the tier and period. SHOW DESCRIBE

The Irreversible Choice

The single most important thing to know before you attach an archival policy.

A table is permanently assigned to its archive tier for its lifetime. You cannot change COOL to COLD or COLD to COOL. Dropping the policy and attaching a different one does not work either — the table remembers. Changing the tier requires contacting Snowflake Support to delete the archived data.

Why It Matters

This is a one-way door taken at attach time, usually by whoever was cleaning up storage that week. Getting it wrong means either paying 4× more than necessary indefinitely, or living with 48-hour retrieval on data that turned out to be needed.

What To Do

Decide the tier with the data owner before attaching, not after. Ask one question: if someone needs a row back, is a 48-hour wait acceptable? If the answer is no, or unknown, choose COOL.

DESCRIBE STORAGE LIFECYCLE POLICY reports archive_tier even after an archival policy has been converted to an expiration-only policy. The tier history is visible — use it to audit what a table is already committed to before you plan a change. DESCRIBE Archive tier limitations

Retrieving Archived Data

Archived rows are not queryable in place. You restore a copy of exactly the rows you want.

-- Look before you pay: row counts and column min/max,
-- with NO retrieval cost.
SELECT SYSTEM$GET_TABLE_ARCHIVE_METADATA('finance.ledger.transactions');

-- Restore only the rows you need into a new table.
CREATE TRANSIENT TABLE finance.ledger.txn_restore
  FROM ARCHIVE OF finance.ledger.transactions
  WHERE TXN_TS BETWEEN '2023-03-15' AND '2023-03-25';

Check Metadata First

SYSTEM$GET_TABLE_ARCHIVE_METADATA returns row counts and column min/max without incurring retrieval cost. Use it to confirm the data is even there before paying to pull it. Function reference

Narrow the WHERE

Retrieval is billed. A precise predicate is the difference between restoring a week and restoring a decade.

Use TRANSIENT

A restore is usually temporary. TRANSIENT avoids Fail-safe storage cost on a table you intend to drop.

COLD Takes Hours

Up to 48 hours, and a maximum of 1 million files per restore operation. Plan around it — this is not an interactive workflow.

Removing a policy from a table does not delete archived data; it remains retrievable. Truncating the table also leaves the archive intact. UNDROP within Time Travel restores archived data along with the table. Retrieving archived data

What Gets Locked

The daily run is not free of side effects on concurrent work.

Why this matters for pipeline design: ingestion keeps working during a policy run, but anything doing merge-style upserts on the same table can block. On a large table the run may span multiple days, widening the window. If a table is both lifecycle-managed and MERGE-updated, expect contention and plan the schedule around it.

Performance guidelines resemble those for row access policies — the expression is evaluated per row, so keep it cheap and aligned with how the table is clustered. Policy behavior and execution

Replication and Cloning

Both behave in ways that surprise people, and both have real DR consequences.

Replication

Policies and their table associations replicate. Archived data does not. After failover, archived data from the source is not available in the target account.

Snowflake never runs secondary policies on secondary tables, even after failover. It pauses execution in the original primary and resumes on failback.

Cloning

Policies are not automatically applied to clones. Archiving from one table in a clone group affects only that table.

The result is copies in both standard and archive tiers — and you pay for both. A clone can quietly undo the saving you were chasing.

Put this in your DR plan explicitly. If a compliance obligation is satisfied by archived rows, that obligation is not met in the failover account, because the archive does not travel. Business Critical customers reviewing retention should confirm where the archived copy actually lives before assuming it is covered. Replication considerations

Where It Does Not Apply

Check this list before promising a customer that lifecycle policies will solve their storage bill.

Shared Tables

Not supported on either side of Snowflake data sharing — neither provider nor consumer tables.

Native Apps

Not supported.

WORM Snapshots

Immutable snapshots cannot be modified after creation, so lifecycle policies do not apply.

Most UDF Types

Python, Java, and Scala UDFs, UDFs with external access, and external functions are all unsupported in the expression.

Row Timestamps

Not supported.

Auto-Refresh Interactive Tables

Interactive tables are supported only when they do not auto-refresh. Anything with a target lag is out.

Two encryption notes worth carrying into a security review: Tri-Secret Secure can protect archived data with regular key rotation, but Snowflake does not rekey archived data. If you suspect key compromise, the documented remedy is to retrieve the archive to a new table (which gets its own key), re-archive it, then drop the original archive. Archival storage policies

Monitoring and Cost

Three cost components, one of which is a penalty most people do not anticipate.

Cost Components

One-time archiving costSame for COOL and COLD
Ongoing archive storageCOLD is 4× cheaper
RetrievalCOLD cheaper per byte, slower
Daily policy executionShared compute, no warehouse
Early-deletion penaltyBilled as if kept the full period

The penalty-bytes trap: archived data deleted before ARCHIVE_FOR_DAYS has elapsed is still subject to storage cost for the remainder of the period. Setting a 6-year COLD period and cleaning up after 6 months does not save 5.5 years of storage. Choose the period deliberately — it is a commitment, not a ceiling. Billing

Before You Recommend It

Size the win first. Establish how much of the table is actually older than the threshold, then apply the tier discount to that share — not to the whole table. Rates are in tables 3(e) and 4(f) of the Service Consumption Table. Consumption table

After You Enable It

Monitor policy runs to confirm rows are actually moving and that large operations are progressing across daily runs rather than stalling. Monitoring

The Tier Is Forever

A table is bound to COOL or COLD for its lifetime. Changing it requires Snowflake Support to delete the archive. Decide before attaching.

COLD Is Not Everywhere

AWS and GCP only. Expiration and COOL cover all three clouds. Check before designing.

The Period Is a Commitment

Deleting early still bills for the remainder. ARCHIVE_FOR_DAYS is not an upper bound you can walk back.

The Archive Does Not Fail Over

Policies replicate; archived data does not. If retention compliance rests on the archive, it is not covered in the target account.
